# Module 17: Load management and WhatsApp charging

Module 9 put two add-ons in the catalogue with nothing behind them: `load_management`, "Load management", and `messaging_charging`, "Charging through a messaging app". Module 2 had already given the Owner `load_management:read`, `load_management:write` and `messaging:manage`, Operations the first two, and the Technician `load_management:read`. This module builds both. A tenant with the first add-on **caps a site's power** under Sites > a site > Load management: a limit in kW, a minimum per charging car, and whether the cap is shared equally or by priority; the worker then **shares the cap out** among the site's chargers every ten seconds, sends each one its cap as an OCPP charging profile, and the page shows what every charger holds and whether it accepted. A tenant with the second add-on **sets its WhatsApp number** under Settings > WhatsApp charging; a driver who messages it a plug's code **gets the link to pay** on the driver app, is **told in the chat** when the charge starts and ends, with the receipt, and can **stop it from the chat**. Module 9's third add-on, `advanced_reports`, got its substance in module 11 (scheduled reports); what else it could hold is in _Not in this module_.

## Design decisions

| Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Load management is static caps per charger, recomputed every ten seconds, not a live control loop.** The worker reads which chargers have a car on them (connector status in a preparing, charging, suspended, finishing or occupied state) and shares the cap: every charging charger gets at least the minimum, idle chargers keep the minimum as a reserve so a car can start before the next round, and what is left is shared equally (water-filling up to each charger's ceiling) or by priority. The caps change only when a car plugs in or out, so a charger hears from the platform a few times an hour, not every second |
| **The cap is a `ChargePointMaxProfile` on connector 0 with one fixed id (1700), one period that never ends, in watts.** Setting the same id again replaces the cap on the charger; `ClearChargingProfile` by that id removes exactly ours and leaves any profile the installer put there. A charger that answers `Unknown` to a clear has nothing to clear, which is fine. Per-connector `TxDefaultProfile`s would let two cars on one charger be split differently, at the price of a profile per plug; not needed yet                                                                                                               |
| **A charger's ceiling is what its connectors add up to unless staff say otherwise.** `charger_load_settings.max_power_kw` overrides the sum of `connectors.max_power_kw`; null on both means no known ceiling and the allocator gives it whatever is left. Priority is 1 to 10 with 5 the default; under `priority` the highest is filled first, under `equal_share` it only breaks ties                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Allocations remember what each charger was told and what it said.** `charger_load_allocations` has one row per charger: the cap, why, whether a car is on it, the command it waits for, whether the charger accepted, when, and the last error. A rejected or timed-out cap is tried again after 30 seconds, then two, five and ten minutes; a charger that boots again gets its cap again, because a reboot can lose profiles. An offline charger's row shows the planned cap and the command goes when it is back                                                                                                                 |
| **Switching the policy off, or losing the add-on, clears the caps.** The round queues a `clear_charging_profile` for every charger with an allocation and deletes the rows, so the chargers are uncapped and the page empty. A charger offline at that moment misses the clear once the queued command expires (five minutes, as any command); it keeps the last cap until the policy is switched on again or someone clears it. Add-on entitlements are checked by the worker itself, since an add-on never appears in a plan's module list                                                                                          |
| **The chat is one conversation per driver's number per channel, with a token that follows the charge.** A plug code gets the driver app's link with `?chat=<link_token>`; the page sends it back as `chat_ref` when it starts the charge, and `ChatChargeLinker` ties the session and the charge key to the conversation inside the same transaction. The worker's `ChatNotifyJob` then says "started" once and "ended, here is your receipt" once, kept once-only by `notified_kinds` on the conversation. A new plug code resets the link                                                                                           |
| **Messages are an outbox the worker sends; the API only writes.** The API answers Meta within milliseconds and never waits on Meta's send endpoint. `MessagingSendJob` sends pending outbound messages every `MESSAGING_INTERVAL_MS`, tries a provider hiccup again after 30 seconds and two minutes, then marks the message failed with the reason, which the portal shows under the conversation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **The provider is behind an interface, like payments.** `MessagingProvider` has `send` and `isEnabled`; `WhatsAppCloudProvider` is Meta's Cloud API (`POST /{phone_number_id}/messages`, a text or an interactive message with up to three reply buttons). `FakeMessagingProvider` records sends for tests. Another app is another class; the texts, the command parser and the outbox do not change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **One Meta app, many tenants.** The platform's `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN` sends for every tenant whose number sits in the platform's WhatsApp Business account; a tenant with its own account saves its own token on its channel, stored as is and never shown again. Meta delivers every number's messages to one webhook, so the channel is found by `phone_number_id` before the tenant is known, with the `messaging_channels_phone_number_lookup` policy under `app.messaging_phone_number_id`, the way module 16 finds a key by its hash                                                                                           |
| **Every delivery is checked against `WHATSAPP_APP_SECRET` over the raw bytes, and every message is handled once.** `X-Hub-Signature-256` is HMAC-SHA256 of the body; a bad one is 400 and nothing is stored. The provider's message id is unique per tenant among inbound rows, so a delivery Meta sends twice gets one reply. Subscribing echoes `hub.challenge` only for our `WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **The chat does four things and nothing else.** A plug (`charge WW-7KQ4M2XR9T/1`, or just the code and the number; the sticker's `wa.me` link fills it in), `stop`, `status`, and help for anything else, with the tenant's greeting the first time. Stopping queues the same RemoteStop the driver's page would (module 7); status reads the linked session. Payment stays on the driver app's page, where the card form is, so no card data ever crosses the chat                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **A paused channel, a missing add-on, and the plan guard.** A channel with `is_active = false` ignores messages and its pending replies wait. A tenant whose add-on ran out gets no replies and its staff pages answer 403 `module_not_in_plan`, as `PermissionGuard` already does for every permission that belongs to a module (module 9). A test message goes through the same outbox, worker and provider, so what staff see is what drivers get                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Every save is a versioned write with an audit entry.** `If-Match` as everywhere, with `0` allowed for the first save of a site's policy or the channel, since they do not exist until then. `load_management.updated` on the site, `messaging.channel_created` and `messaging.channel_updated` on the channel, with the policy and the number before and after                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

## Running it

Apply the migrations as always (`npm run db:migrate` or `./scripts/up.sh`, which runs them). New settings: the worker's `LOAD_MANAGEMENT_INTERVAL_MS` (10000), `MESSAGING_INTERVAL_MS` (10000), `DRIVER_URL` (for the links in chats), `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN` and `WHATSAPP_API_URL`; the API's `WHATSAPP_APP_SECRET`, `WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN` and `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN`. The seed gives Acme both add-ons and caps Riverside at 30 kW.

1. Log in to the portal as `owner@acme.test`, open **Sites > Riverside Car Park** and follow **Load management**: the policy is on at 30 kW; the chargers show no cap yet because none is online.
2. Start the simulator as the Riverside charger (`npm run sim -- --identity WW-7KQ4M2XR9T`, as in module 4). Within ten seconds the page shows the charger's cap, "Cap applied", and the simulator logs `power cap set to … W`. Plug a car in (`start 1` in the simulator): the cap moves up to the limit, since one car has the site to itself. Change the priority or the limit and save: the next round sends the new caps.
3. Switch the policy off: the simulator logs `power cap removed` and the page shows no caps.
4. For WhatsApp you need a Meta app with the WhatsApp product, a test number, and a public address for the API (a tunnel does for a try). Put the app secret, a verify token of your choosing and the number's access token into `deploy/.env` as `WHATSAPP_*` and restart. Under **Settings > WhatsApp charging** enter the phone number id and the number as printed, save, and copy the webhook address; in the Meta app, set it as the callback with your verify token and subscribe to `messages`. Press **Send test** with your own number: the worker sends it within ten seconds and the conversation appears in the list.
5. Message the number `charge WW-7KQ4M2XR9T/1`: the reply carries the driver app's link. Open it and start the charge as in module 6; the chat gets "Charging has started" with Stop and Status buttons, and "Your charge has ended" with the receipt link after you stop, from the chat or the simulator.
6. Log in as `multi@demo.test` in Volt Hotels: neither page is offered, and the API answers 403 `module_not_in_plan`.
